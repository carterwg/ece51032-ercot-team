Part 1: electrical bus groups and measured interactions

101 candidates, 120 periods, 9 valid background scenarios, 96 sampled unordered pairs, 384 directional load tests. Solver: highs. All candidates participate in interaction tests.

Transmission groups may overlap. Every bus also shares the system generation ceiling. PTDF cohorts are a refinement hypothesis; use the validation status, not the cohort ID alone, when selecting Part 2 experiments. An unshared line signature does not imply independent capacity.

What was tested

Each rival requests at most 500 MW; 2,000 MW backgrounds represent four teams. Five-team backgrounds total 2,500 MW. Background withdrawals are fixed and uncurtailed, with no onsite generators or storage. Capacity limits are checked over every period. Actual 500 MW economic-dispatch observations use periods (13, 25, 37, 61, 85, 108, 109, 110); near-limit means at least 95% loading with a positive aligned flow increment. These are electrical diagnostics, not official allocation or scores.

Interaction validation

For a target j, I=(HC_j−HC_j after q at i)/HC_j. We separately remove the unavoidable loss imposed by the shared generation ceiling: max(0, HC_j−(system headroom−q)). Excess/q measures additional transmission interaction. The pair sample is fixed using the original seven backgrounds before adding the two five-rival cases. Means are descriptive for this deterministic, coverage-balanced sample; directions and load sizes are dependent.

Use in Part 2

Use measured interactions and group evidence to choose matched concentrated and diversified plans. Keep the total request, generation/storage mix, opponent scenarios, and S1-to-S2 history matched. Validate with the full game evaluator: the present groups do not establish competitor choice probabilities, allocation resilience, or economic superiority.
